# Anti-Patterns to Flag and Fix

These common failures destroy MBB-quality decks. Call them out explicitly and propose concrete fixes.

## Narrative & Structure

- **The slow reveal** — Saving the recommendation for the last slide instead of leading with it.
- **The false executive summary** — An “overview” or agenda slide that does not actually summarize findings and recommendations.
- **The orphan slide** — A slide that does not connect to the ones before or after it.
- **The frankendeck** — Slides pulled from different sources with inconsistent terminology, formatting, and narrative threads.
- **Non-MECE structure** — Overlapping or incomplete categories in arguments or sections.

## Titles

- **Topic titles** — “Revenue Analysis”, “Market Landscape”, “Implementation Plan”. Rewrite as complete takeaway sentences.
- **Title echo** — Title simply restates the chart axis or a label already visible on the slide.
- **Vague titles** — Titles that fail the “so what?” test.

## Slide Content

- **One-message violation** — Multiple distinct insights forced onto a single slide.
- **Bullet point disease** — Slides that are primarily nested bullets with little or no visual evidence.
- **The data dump** — Dense numbers or tables with no narrative insight or clear takeaway.
- **Chart soup** — Multiple charts on one slide, each making a different point.
- **Decorative clutter** — Icons, shapes, or colors that do not convey information.

## Evidence & Credibility

- **Missing sources** — Data points without citation.
- **Unsupported claims** — Strong statements with no evidence on the slide or in the appendix.
- **Single-source over-reliance** or inflated / non-conservative assumptions without disclosure.

## Visual

- **Inconsistent formatting** — Varying title sizes, fonts, colors, or alignment across slides.
- **Cramped or sparse layouts** — Poor use of whitespace.
- **Low-contrast text** or hard-to-read chart labels.
- **3D effects, heavy gradients, or chart junk**.

## Healthcare-Specific

- **Jargon overload** without translation for mixed audiences.
- **Clinically imprecise language** or frameworks applied incorrectly.
- **Patient journey maps that are decorative rather than insight-driven**.

When any of these appear, flag them clearly, show the concrete problem, and provide a specific rewritten title, restructured slide, or design fix.

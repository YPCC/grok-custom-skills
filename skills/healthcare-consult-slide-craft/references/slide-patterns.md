# Common Slide Patterns

Use these patterns as starting points. Always adapt to the specific action title and data.

## 1. Executive Summary (SCR)

- Action title states the overall recommendation or governing insight.
- Body uses bold-claim + indented evidence format:
  - **Bold sentence** = key claim
  - Supporting bullets or short data points underneath
- Structure: Situation (brief) → Complication → Resolution (majority of the slide).
- Must stand alone. A busy executive should understand the entire argument from this one slide.

## 2. Recommendation / Resolution Pillar

- Action title = the specific recommendation or pillar.
- 2–4 MECE supporting arguments or proof points.
- Single strong exhibit (chart, framework, or process) preferred.
- Clear implication / “so what”.

## 3. Data / Evidence Slide

- Action title states the insight the data proves.
- One primary chart or table.
- Minimal annotation or callout that highlights the key number or trend.
- Source at bottom.

## 4. Market Sizing (Healthcare)

- Action title states the size insight (e.g., “The addressable U.S. digital therapeutics market will reach $X by 2028”).
- TAM / SAM / SOM structure preferred.
- Show both top-down and bottom-up when possible; reconcile large differences.
- Methodology and key assumptions noted.
- Sources mandatory.

## 5. Patient / Care Journey or Process

- Action title states the key insight about the journey or process.
- Horizontal or vertical stages with thin teal connectors.
- Each stage: short label + 1–2 critical insights or pain points / opportunities.
- Keep visual weight balanced.

## 6. Status / Implementation Update

- Action title states overall status insight.
- RAG indicators (Green / Amber / Red) used sparingly and clearly defined.
- Key metrics, recent accomplishments, upcoming milestones, risks, and decisions needed.
- Transparent about problems.

## 7. Roadmap / Next Steps

- Action title states the path forward or key sequencing insight.
- Phased timeline or clear ownership + timing.
- Explicit “ask” or decision required when relevant.

## 8. Framework / Matrix (2×2, etc.)

- Action title states the strategic implication of the framework.
- Clean axes and quadrant labels.
- Items placed precisely; call out the most important quadrant or movement.

## 9. One-Pager / Leave-Behind

- Distills the entire argument onto a single slide or page.
- Strong action-style headline.
- 3–5 key points with supporting data.
- Clear visual hierarchy and call to action.

## Healthcare-Specific Notes

- Prefer clinically or operationally precise language.
- When using clinical or regulatory terms, keep them accurate and accessible to mixed executive audiences.
- Patient-centric framing is often powerful when relevant, but never at the expense of the action title or MECE structure.
- For research administration and grant-related topics (e.g., agentic AI support for Principal Investigators), see the worked example in `references/examples.md`.

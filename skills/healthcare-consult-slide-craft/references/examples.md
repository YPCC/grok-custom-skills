# Worked Examples

Concrete illustrations of how to apply the skill. Use these as patterns, not rigid templates.

## Example 1: Agentic AI Solution for Principal Investigators Submitting Grant Proposals

**Brief (illustrative)**  
A research institution or academic medical center wants a consulting-style recommendation on deploying an agentic AI system to help Principal Investigators (PIs) prepare and submit competitive grant proposals more efficiently and with higher quality.

**Audience**  
Research leadership, CTO / digital innovation, research administration, and senior PIs.

**Governing decision the deck must drive**  
Approve a pilot of an agentic AI grant-support platform (or choose among build / buy / partner options).

### Sample Ghost Deck (Action Titles Only)

1. **Cover** — Agentic AI for Grant Proposal Excellence | [Institution] Research Strategy
2. **Executive Summary** — Deploying an agentic AI co-pilot will cut average proposal preparation time by 40% while raising first-round success rates through structured, evidence-backed writing support
3. **Situation** — Principal Investigators currently spend 120–200 hours per major proposal on administrative and drafting tasks that do not require their unique scientific judgment
4. **Complication** — Rising competition, increasingly complex multi-investigator and multi-institution requirements, and administrative burden are reducing both proposal volume and quality
5. **Root causes are MECE and addressable** — Fragmented tools, lack of institutional knowledge reuse, inconsistent compliance checking, and absence of real-time strategic feedback
6. **An agentic AI system can own the end-to-end non-scientific workflow** — From opportunity matching and outline generation through compliance, budgeting support, and institutional review packaging
7. **Three high-value agent capabilities deliver the majority of impact** — Opportunity intelligence agent, structured drafting & evidence agent, and compliance + packaging agent
8. **Expected impact is material and measurable** — 40% reduction in PI time, 15–25% lift in submission quality scores, faster institutional review cycles
9. **Recommended path is a 6-month controlled pilot with clear success metrics** — Start with NIH R01 / equivalent mechanisms, measure time, quality, and PI satisfaction
10. **Roadmap and ask** — Approve pilot funding and cross-functional steering committee by [date]; full scale decision after 90-day evaluation
11. **Appendix** — Detailed architecture, risk register, build-vs-buy analysis, sample agent interaction flows

### How the principles are applied

- **SCR arc**: Situation (current PI burden) → Complication (rising complexity & competition) → Resolution (agentic AI co-pilot + pilot recommendation).
- **Action titles**: Every title is a complete takeaway sentence. Reading titles 2–10 alone conveys the full argument.
- **MECE**: Root causes and agent capabilities are structured without overlap.
- **One message per slide**: Each slide advances exactly one claim.
- **Exhibit ideas**:
  - Current-state process map with time hotspots
  - Agentic architecture diagram (orchestrator + specialized agents)
  - Before/after time allocation for a typical R01
  - Pilot success metrics dashboard mock-up
  - Simple 2×2 of build / buy / partner options

### Suggested slide patterns to use

- Executive Summary (SCR + bold-claim format)
- Process / journey map (current vs future PI workflow)
- Framework for agent capabilities (three capability pillars)
- Impact quantification (data slide with projected time and quality lift)
- Roadmap + clear ask

This example demonstrates how the skill handles a modern digital-health / research-administration topic while staying rigorously MBB in structure and visual discipline.
